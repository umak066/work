## Phase 1: Concept & Market Research

**Timeline:** Months 1–2

### 1.1 Define Your Concept & Differentiators

- **Concept direction** (inspired by Jabal Coffee House):
  - Middle Eastern / Mediterranean specialty coffeehouse
  - Specialty-grade espresso + culturally unique beverages (Turkish coffee, cardamom lattes, saffron drinks)
  - Artisan pastries with a Middle Eastern twist (baklava, ka'ak, manakeesh, knafeh)
  - Warm, community-gathering-place atmosphere with cultural design elements
- **Unique Selling Propositions (USPs):**
  - Cultural authenticity not found at Starbucks or Biggby
  - Specialty-grade coffee (not commodity beans)
  - Handmade, in-house pastries and light food
  - Community space: hookah lounge area (if legally permissible — see permits), board games, live music nights
- **Brand identity:** Name, logo, color palette, typography, interior design mood board
- **Service model decision:** Counter service, table service, or hybrid

### 1.2 Market Research — White Lake Specific

- **Demographics:**
  - White Lake Township population: ~30,000
  - Median household income: ~$75,000–$85,000
  - Primarily suburban, family-oriented community
  - Mix of younger families and retirees
  - Growing diversity in Oakland County
- **Competitive landscape — map every competitor within 10-mile radius:**
  - Starbucks (Highland Rd / M-59 corridor)
  - Biggby Coffee (multiple Oakland County locations)
  - Tim Hortons
  - Local independents in Milford, Highland, Waterford, Commerce Township
  - Identify what they do well and where they fall short
- **Customer research:**
  - Survey potential customers (Facebook community groups, Nextdoor White Lake)
  - Visit top-rated coffee shops in metro Detroit for inspiration
  - Identify peak hours pattern: early morning commuters on M-59, midday work-from-home crowd, evening socializing
- **Demand validation:**
  - Test concept at local farmers' markets or pop-up events
  - Run a small social media campaign to gauge interest
  - Talk to local business owners about foot traffic patterns

### 1.3 Site Visits & Competitor Analysis

- Visit Jabal Coffee House in person — document layout, menu, pricing, service flow, ambiance
- Visit 5–10 other successful independent coffee shops in SE Michigan
- Create a comparison matrix: pricing, menu breadth, seating capacity, hours, reviews

## Phase 3: Legal & Entity Setup

**Timeline:** Months 2–4

### 3.1 Business Entity Formation

1. **Choose entity type:** LLC is recommended (liability protection + pass-through taxation)
2. **Register with Michigan LARA:**
   - File Articles of Organization online at michigan.gov/lara
   - Filing fee: ~$50
   - Choose a registered agent (can be yourself at a Michigan address)
3. **Obtain Federal EIN:**
   - Apply free at irs.gov
   - Required for bank accounts, hiring, and tax filings
4. **Operating Agreement:** Draft an LLC operating agreement (even for single-member LLCs)
   - Defines ownership percentages, profit distribution, management structure
   - Critical if you have business partners
5. **DBA / Assumed Name (if needed):**
   - If your trade name differs from your LLC name
   - File with Oakland County Clerk — ~$10

### 3.2 Tax Registrations

- **Michigan Sales Tax License:** Register with Michigan Department of Treasury
  - Michigan sales tax rate: **6%** on prepared food and beverages
  - File monthly or quarterly returns
- **Withholding Tax:** Register for Michigan income tax withholding (if hiring employees)
- **Federal taxes:** Quarterly estimated tax payments (Form 1040-ES for sole proprietors/LLC members)
- **Michigan Corporate Income Tax / Flow-Through Entity Tax** (depends on structure)

### 3.3 Legal Counsel

- Hire a local business attorney for:
  - Lease review and negotiation (this alone can save you thousands)
  - Operating agreement drafting
  - Franchise/licensing advice (if applicable)
  - Trademark search and filing for your business name
- **Budget:** $2,000 – $5,000 for initial legal setup
- **Recommendation:** Find an attorney experienced in restaurant/food-service businesses

### 3.4 Intellectual Property

- **Trademark your business name and logo:** File with USPTO (~$250–$350 per class)
- **Domain name:** Secure your .com domain immediately
- **Social media handles:** Claim your name on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X before someone else does

## Phase 4: Location & Lease Negotiation

**Timeline:** Months 3–5

### 4.1 Location Criteria

- **Ideal size:** 1,200 – 2,500 sq ft
- **Must-haves:**
  - High visibility from a main road (M-59 / Highland Rd corridor is prime)
  - Adequate parking (minimum 15–20 spaces; customers won't walk far)
  - Existing grease trap and commercial plumbing (saves $10K–$30K in buildout)
  - Adequate electrical capacity (commercial espresso machines need 220V, 30+ amps)
  - ADA accessibility
  - Proximity to residential neighborhoods, schools, or shopping centers
- **Nice-to-haves:**
  - Drive-thru lane or potential to add one (huge revenue driver in suburban MI)
  - Patio/outdoor seating space
  - Previous restaurant/café tenant (reduces buildout cost significantly)
  - End-cap or standalone unit for maximum signage visibility

### 4.2 Specific White Lake Areas to Consider

| Area | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| M-59 (Highland Rd) corridor | Highest traffic, most visibility | Higher rent, more competition |
| Bogie Lake Rd area | Growing residential, less competition | Lower traffic volume |
| Elizabeth Lake Rd | Near Waterford border, good traffic | Farther from White Lake center |
| Union Lake Rd / Cooley Lake Rd | Near Commerce Township border, busy | May pull customers from Commerce |
| White Lake Village Plaza area | Existing retail foot traffic | Dependent on co-tenants |

### 4.3 Lease Negotiation — Critical Points

- **Lease type:** Negotiate a **NNN (Triple Net)** or **Modified Gross** lease — understand the difference
  - NNN: you pay base rent + property taxes + insurance + maintenance (common in MI retail)
  - Modified Gross: landlord covers some expenses — negotiate hard for this
- **Negotiate these terms:**
  - **Tenant Improvement Allowance (TI):** $10–$40/sq ft for buildout — ask for this first
  - **Free rent period:** 2–4 months during buildout (you shouldn't pay rent while constructing)
  - **Gradual rent escalation:** cap annual increases at 2–3%
  - **Exclusive use clause:** no other coffee shop in the same shopping center
  - **Kick-out clause:** option to exit if revenue doesn't hit a threshold after 18–24 months
  - **Renewal options:** at least two 5-year renewal options
  - **Signage rights:** right to install exterior signage, pylon sign inclusion
  - **Hours of operation:** confirm no restrictions on early morning (5–6 AM) opening
  - **Sublease / assignment rights:** ability to sell the business and transfer the lease
- **Lease term:** 5 years initial with two 5-year renewal options is standard
- **Expected rent:** $12 – $22/sq ft/year in Oakland County (~$1,500–$4,500/month for 1,500 sq ft)
- **ALWAYS have your attorney review the lease before signing**

### 4.4 Letter of Intent (LOI)

- Submit an LOI before negotiating the full lease
- Include: proposed rent, lease term, TI allowance request, free rent period, intended use
- This is non-binding but sets the framework for negotiation

### 5.2 Health Department Requirements (Oakland County)

- **Pre-construction plan review:** Submit architectural plans to Oakland County Health Division BEFORE starting buildout
- **Required equipment/layout elements:**
  - 3-compartment sink for manual dishwashing
  - Separate handwashing sinks (minimum 1 in prep area, 1 in restroom)
  - Commercial dishwasher (if high volume)
  - Adequate refrigeration with thermometers
  - Proper food storage (6 inches off floor, labeled, dated)
  - Separate raw/cooked food storage areas
  - Smooth, washable floor/wall/ceiling surfaces in food prep areas
  - Adequate ventilation / hood system (if cooking)
  - Employee restroom separate from customer restroom (if space allows)
  - Mop sink
  - Pest control plan
- **Inspections:** Initial pre-opening inspection + routine inspections (typically 1–3 per year)

### 5.3 Michigan-Specific Food Service Regulations

- **Michigan Food Law (Act 92 of 2000):** Governs all food service establishments
- **Michigan Modified Food Code:** Based on FDA Food Code — covers food temps, hygiene, storage
- **Key requirements:**
  - Person in Charge (PIC) must be present during all hours of operation
  - PIC must hold a ServSafe Manager Certification or equivalent
  - All employees must complete food handler training within 30 days of hire
  - HACCP plan required if doing any complex food prep (smoking, curing, sprouting)
  - Allergen awareness training for all staff
- **Cottage Food Law does NOT apply** to coffee shops — you need a full food service license

### 5.4 Liquor License Considerations (If Applicable)

- Michigan has a quota system for full liquor licenses — they're expensive and scarce
- **Alternative options for a coffee shop:**
  - **Small Winemaker License:** if only serving Michigan wine
  - **Small Distiller License:** for Michigan spirits
  - **Micro Brewer License:** for Michigan craft beer
  - **Specially Designated Merchant (SDM) License:** for beer and wine only (more obtainable)
- **Cost:** $600–$1,500 annually for basic beer/wine; full liquor can run $50K+ for a quota license
- **Recommended:** Start without alcohol, add beer/wine after you're established

### 5.5 Tobacco / Hookah Regulations

- If offering hookah (common in Middle Eastern coffeehouses):
  - Michigan Clean Indoor Air Act restricts indoor smoking — hookah lounges must meet specific exemptions
  - May need a **Tobacco Specialty Retail License** from Michigan LARA
  - Local ordinances in White Lake Township may further restrict or prohibit hookah
  - **Check with White Lake Township before committing to this in your concept**
  - Must be 21+ area
  - Separate ventilation system required

## Phase 6: Buildout, Design & Equipment

**Timeline:** Months 5–8

### 6.1 Design & Architecture

- **Hire a commercial architect** experienced in restaurant/café design
  - Budget: $3,000 – $12,000 for plans
  - They'll handle ADA compliance, health code layout requirements, and building code
- **Interior design direction** (Jabal-inspired):
  - Warm earth tones, natural materials (wood, stone, brass accents)
  - Middle Eastern geometric patterns, arched doorways, mosaic tiles
  - Comfortable seating mix: sofas, armchairs, traditional low seating, standard tables
  - Feature wall with cultural artwork or calligraphy
  - Warm, ambient lighting (avoid harsh fluorescents)
  - Plants and greenery
- **Floor plan must include:**
  - Customer entry and queuing area
  - Service counter and espresso bar (visible to customers — theater of coffee making)
  - Pastry/food display case
  - POS station(s)
  - Seating area (plan for 30–60 seats depending on size)
  - Kitchen/prep area (if serving food beyond pre-made items)
  - Storage room (dry goods, paper products, cleaning supplies)
  - Walk-in cooler or reach-in refrigeration
  - Office / employee break area
  - Customer restrooms (ADA compliant)
  - Mop closet / utility area
  - Outdoor patio (if space allows — huge draw in Michigan summers)

### 6.2 Construction & Buildout

- **Hire a licensed commercial contractor** — get 3 bids minimum
- **Key buildout elements:**
  - Demolition of existing layout (if needed)
  - Framing and drywall
  - Plumbing rough-in and finish (water lines, drains, grease trap connection)
  - Electrical (220V circuits for espresso machine, sufficient 120V outlets, proper lighting circuits)
  - HVAC modifications (adequate ventilation for kitchen, comfortable dining area)
  - Flooring: non-slip, commercial-grade in kitchen; aesthetic in dining area
  - Hood and exhaust system (required if cooking with grease/heat)
  - Paint, trim, and finishes
  - Countertop fabrication and installation
  - Restroom construction/renovation
  - ADA ramp, door widths, restroom grab bars
  - Fire suppression system (may need modification)
- **Budget:** $50,000 – $150,000
- **Timeline:** 6–12 weeks typical for a moderate buildout
- **Critical path:** Permits must be approved BEFORE construction begins

## Risk Analysis & Contingency Planning

### 13.1 Risk Matrix

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slow initial customer traction** | Medium | High | Pre-opening marketing, soft opening buzz, loyalty incentives, grand opening event |
| **Key equipment failure** | Medium | High | Warranty coverage, backup manual brewing methods, emergency repair fund, local technician on speed dial |
| **Staff turnover** | High | Medium | Competitive pay, positive culture, cross-training all staff, documentation of all procedures |
| **Supply chain disruption** | Low-Medium | Medium | Multiple vendor relationships, 2-week buffer inventory, shelf-stable alternatives |
| **Negative online reviews** | Medium | High | Respond to every review within 24 hours, resolve issues immediately, encourage happy customers to review |
| **Seasonal revenue dips** | High | Medium | Seasonal menu changes, event programming, catering push during slow months |
| **New competitor opens nearby** | Medium | Medium | Strong brand loyalty, unique cultural concept, superior quality, community relationships |
| **Rising ingredient costs** | Medium | Medium | Annual menu price adjustments (3–5%), renegotiate vendor contracts, menu engineering to push high-margin items |
| **Health code violation** | Low | High | Rigorous daily cleaning checklist, regular self-inspections, ongoing staff training |
| **Economic recession** | Low | High | Build cash reserve (3–6 months), value menu options, focus on coffee (affordable luxury) |
| **Lease non-renewal** | Low | High | Long-term lease with renewal options, good landlord relationship, keep space well-maintained |
| **Food safety incident** | Low | Very High | Strict HACCP protocols, allergen training, liability insurance, documented procedures |
| **Natural disaster / fire** | Very Low | Very High | Business interruption insurance, property insurance, emergency procedures, data backup |
| **Partner/ownership dispute** | Low-Medium | Very High | Clear operating agreement, buy-sell provisions, defined roles and responsibilities |

### 13.2 Emergency Preparedness

- **Equipment failure plan:** Identify backup brewing methods, list of 24-hour repair services
- **Power outage plan:** Battery backup for POS? Cash-only procedures? At what point do you close?
- **Water outage plan:** Cannot operate food service without water — closing procedure
- **Staffing emergency plan:** Who fills in when someone calls out? Owner as backup barista
- **Data backup:** Cloud-based POS (Square/Toast) automatically backs up sales data
- **Cash emergency:** Minimum $5,000 accessible emergency fund

### 13.3 Exit Strategy

- **Scenario A — Sell the business:** Build transferable systems, strong financials, assignable lease
- **Scenario B — Close the business:** Lease termination clause, equipment liquidation plan, wind-down timeline
- **Scenario C — Partnership buyout:** Buy-sell agreement with predetermined valuation method
