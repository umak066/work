## PHASE 2: IMPORT PROCESS — GETTING RAW MATERIAL FROM CHINA (Weeks 4–12)

### Step 6: Verify Your Chinese Supplier

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **GACC Registration** | Your Chinese vendor MUST be registered with China's General Administration of Customs (GACC) as an approved exporter of animal products to India. **Ask them for their GACC number.** |
| **Health/Sanitary Certificate** | The vendor must provide a health certificate from GACC for every shipment |
| **Certificate of Analysis (CoA)** | Lab report showing: microbial counts, heavy metals (lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury), moisture, protein content |
| **Manufacturing License** | Copy of their Chinese food manufacturing license |
| **Prior India export history** | Ask if they've exported to India before — experienced exporters will have smoother paperwork |

### Step 7: HS Code Classification & Customs Duty

**Most likely HS Codes:**
| HS Code | Description | Estimated Total Duty |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| **1603.00** | Extracts and juices of meat | 30–60% total |
| **2106.90** | Food preparations not elsewhere specified | 50–100%+ total |
| **3504.00** | Peptones and derivatives (for collagen peptides) | 30–50% |

**Duty structure breakdown (example for HS 2106.90):**
| Duty Component | Rate |
|----------------|------|
| Basic Customs Duty (BCD) | 30–50% |
| Social Welfare Surcharge | 10% of BCD |
| IGST (on import) | 18% |
| Agriculture Infrastructure Cess | Variable (0–100%) |
| **Total landed cost multiplier** | **1.5x – 2.5x of CIF price** |

> **CRITICAL**: At $12/kg CIF price (≈₹1,020/kg), your landed cost after duties could be **₹1,530 – ₹2,550/kg**. This makes or breaks your unit economics. Get exact HS code classification from a licensed customs broker BEFORE committing to large orders.

**Action:** Hire a customs broker — budget ₹5,000–₹20,000 for consultation. Check ICEGATE portal: https://icegate.gov.in

### Step 8: Animal Quarantine Clearance (AQCS)

Since this is an animal-derived product:
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Import Permit from AQCS** | Must be obtained BEFORE shipment leaves China |
| **Authority** | Animal Quarantine & Certification Service, Dept. of Animal Husbandry |
| **Designated ports only** | Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Bangalore, Hyderabad |
| **Physical inspection** | Mandatory at port of entry |
| **Timeline** | 2–4 weeks for permit; 5–15 days clearance at port per consignment |

### Step 9: Import Clearance Flow
```
China vendor ships → Arrives at Indian port → AQCS physical inspection
→ FSSAI import clearance (FSS Online portal) → Lab testing at FSSAI-notified lab
→ Test report (5-15 working days) → Customs clearance → Release to you
```

**Per-consignment costs:**
| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Lab testing (FSSAI) | ₹10,000–₹30,000 per consignment |
| Customs broker fees | ₹5,000–₹15,000 per consignment |
| Port handling + demurrage (if delays) | ₹10,000–₹50,000 |
| Freight (China to India, sea, per kg) | ₹15–₹40/kg depending on volume |

### Step 10: First Import — Start with a Small Commercial Shipment
- Order 50–100 kg as your first import after all licenses are ready
- Budget ₹1.5–₹3.0 Lakh for the first shipment (landed cost)
- Use this batch for formulation R&D and regulatory testing

## PHASE 6: PACKAGING DESIGN & LABELING (Weeks 10–16)

### Step 19: Sachet & Box Design

**Your planned format:** 20–25 sachets × 15g each per box = 300–375g per box

**Sachet requirements:**
- Material: Laminated film (PET/Alu/PE) for moisture and oxygen barrier
- Size: ~80mm × 120mm (for 15g powder)
- Nitrogen flushed (recommended for shelf life)
- Tear notch for easy opening

### Step 20: FSSAI Mandatory Label Information

Every sachet MUST display:
1. **Product Name** — e.g., "Chicken Bone Broth Masala Powder"
2. **Non-Veg Symbol** — Brown/dark red circle inside a square (MANDATORY for chicken)
3. **Ingredients list** — Descending order by weight
4. **Nutritional Information** — Per 100g AND per serving (15g)
5. **Net Weight** — "15g" or "NET WT. 15g"
6. **Date of Manufacture** & **Best Before** date
7. **Batch/Lot Number**
8. **FSSAI License Number** — 14-digit number
9. **Name & Address** — Your company (Indian entity)
10. **Country of Origin** of raw material — "Contains ingredient sourced from China" or as required
11. **Allergen Declaration** — "Contains: Chicken" (poultry allergen)
12. **Directions for Use** — e.g., "Dissolve in 200ml hot water, stir well"
13. **Storage Instructions** — "Store in cool, dry place"
14. **Customer Care** — Phone/email

**For small sachets (<100 cm² surface area):** Only product name, net qty, batch no., best before, and FSSAI license number required ON the sachet. Other details can be on the outer carton.

You are selling pre-packaged commodities with declared quantity (15g sachets). This triggers the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009** and the **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011**.

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Legal Metrology Registration** | Mandatory for any manufacturer/importer/packer of pre-packaged goods |
| **Authority** | Department of Consumer Affairs, State Legal Metrology Department |
| **What it covers** | Accuracy of declared net weight, MRP printing, manufacturing date, etc. |
| **Penalty for non-compliance** | ₹25,000–₹1,00,000 fine; goods can be seized |
| **Cost** | ₹2,000–₹5,000 (application fee) |
| **Timeline** | 15–30 days |

**MRP Rules:**
- MRP must be printed on each sachet AND the outer carton
- MRP is inclusive of all taxes (GST included)
- Retail price cannot exceed MRP
- If selling at different prices online vs offline, you may need separate MRP for each channel

### Step 33: Payment to Chinese Supplier — FEMA Compliance
| Method | Details | Risk |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Wire Transfer (TT)** | Most common for small orders; bank charges ₹500–₹2,000 per transaction | No protection if supplier doesn't deliver |

**RBI/FEMA Requirements:**
- All foreign exchange transactions must comply with **Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999**
- Your bank handles the forex conversion; keep your AD (Authorized Dealer) bank informed
- **Form A1** must be filed for import payments exceeding specified limits
- Keep all import documentation (Bill of Entry, Invoice, shipping docs) for 5+ years — RBI can audit

### Step 38: Incoming Raw Material Quality Control

### Step 47: Breakeven Analysis

**Assumptions (Toll Manufacturing Route):**
- Fixed costs (one-time setup): ₹6,00,000 (low end)
- Monthly fixed costs: ₹30,000 (rent, salary, admin, insurance)
- Variable cost per box (25 sachets): ₹765
- Selling price per box (net of GST, net of channel commission at 20% blended): ₹1,018

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Contribution per box | ₹1,018 – ₹765 = **₹253** |
| Monthly fixed costs | ₹30,000 |
| Boxes to break even per month (ongoing) | 30,000 / 253 = **~119 boxes/month** |
| Sachets to break even per month | 119 × 25 = **~2,975 sachets/month** |
| Revenue at breakeven | 119 × ₹1,271 MRP = **~₹1,51,000/month** |
| Initial investment recovery (at 100 boxes/month profit) | ₹6,00,000 / (100 × ₹253) = **~24 months** |

> **Reality check:** Selling 119 boxes/month = ~4 boxes/day. On Amazon, this is achievable within 6–12 months with good reviews and some marketing spend.
